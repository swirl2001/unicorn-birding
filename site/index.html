What's a unicorn birding day?

The day Battery Godfrey lights up with the most species: migrants passing through, seabirds streaming past the Gate, everything at once.

We're studying 10 years of eBird checklists from Battery Godfrey, adjusted for how many birders were out, to learn which weather brings the biggest days. The forecast switches on as soon as that's done.

Notes on the data

Bird history

  • Source: every eBird checklist at Presidio–Battery Godfrey (hotspot L999240), 2016–2025: 944 days with at least one checklist.
  • Counting species: subspecies and forms count toward their species; "sp.", slash and hybrid entries don't. Feral Rock Pigeon counts, as on eBird.
  • Fair comparison: more birders find more species, so each day's total is adjusted for the number of checklists and observers, and for the time of year.
  • Unicorn day: the top 5% of days for variety at a normal effort (3 checklists by 3 birders): about 77+ species. About 5% of birded days qualify.

Weather history (all local, no SFO)

  • Fort Point station (NOAA NDBC FTPC1, about 1 mile away): wind, gusts, direction, pressure and air temperature.
  • Pier 17 (NOAA): morning visibility, for fog. SF Bar buoy (NDBC 46237): ocean waves.
  • Rain at Battery Godfrey from Open-Meteo's historical archive (modeled), since Fort Point has no rain gauge.
  • Time windows: "overnight" is 8 pm to 6 am, when migrants fly; "morning" is 6 to 11 am; the high is the warmest reading from 6 am to 6 pm.

What predicts a big day

  • Time of year matters most: late September and October lead, then April and May.
  • That day: warm, dry, a rising barometer, and wind off the land overnight (north to east in fall, south in spring).
  • The lead-up: big days often come right after a warm-up of a few degrees and after rain in the previous three days that has cleared out, which can release migrants held back by the storm. Rain the night before hurts.
  • No help: a flip from onshore to offshore wind, south winds on the nights before, and the three-day pressure trend added nothing once the rest was known.

The model and how good it is

  • A logistic regression (a standard statistical model, not AI) learns how much each factor above raises or lowers the odds of a unicorn day.
  • Tested on days it hadn't seen (5-fold cross-validation): its top 5% picks were unicorns 23% of the time, versus 5% for a random birded day.
  • Its highest chances run a little high: days it scored around 30% turned out to be unicorns about 22% of the time. Luck and who shows up matter a lot.

The live forecast

  • Runs automatically at about 5:40 am and 5:40 pm Pacific on GitHub Actions.
  • Overnight wind: National Weather Service hourly forecast over the Presidio. Temperature, pressure, rain, gusts, visibility and sunrise: Open-Meteo at Fort Point.
  • Inputs are capped at the range seen in the history, so a freak forecast can't push the chance off the charts.
  • Email alerts go out once per date when a day within 48 hours reaches 25% (about 5× a normal day).

Record days

  • Checklists, counts and birder names are from eBird. "Notable" bullets are written from the birders' own checklist notes.
  • Weather for 2016–2025 is from the stations above. For 2026 it's measured Fort Point data (NDBC monthly and real-time files), with rain modeled.

Bird history: eBird, Cornell Lab of Ornithology, from the checklists of everyone who birds Battery Godfrey. Weather: National Weather Service, NOAA and Open-Meteo.

Chances are estimates, not guarantees. Birds do what they want.
